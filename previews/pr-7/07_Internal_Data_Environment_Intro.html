We sell US and EU frontier labs two things: environments where a model acts and is checked, and the data those environments produce. Everything is sorted by one question — where does the feedback come from? — and placed on one loop: Hypothesis → Synthesis → Characterisation → Analysis. First physical scope: liquid and liquid-solid chemistry, catalogued by application (coatings, displays, energy storage, photovoltaics).

1. Three kinds, by where the feedback comes from

1b. The fidelity ladder: F0 to F5

Fidelity answers one question: how close to physical reality is the thing that checks the model? Higher means harder for a buyer to rebuild, and so more valuable. Simulation is split into three levels because a neural surrogate, a semi-empirical calculation and DFT differ by orders of magnitude in cost and in how well they hold up.

What this means for the Computational kind. The data and the simulators are free; nobody sells them as verified RL environments. So what we sell at F3 is the task, the verifier and its anti-hacking report, not the calculations. Price it as a volume product, and use it to bring buyers up the ladder to F4–F5, where nothing open competes.

1c. Anatomy: what is inside an environment, and what counts as data

An environment is something a model can act in and be scored by. It has five parts: task set, interface, world, verifier, card. A dataset with no interface is data, however large. A verifier is a scoring system, not one program: rubric and checker produce the reward; noise model, coverage map and anti-hacking set say how far that reward can be trusted.

Trustworthiness suite — future SKU for safety teams

The current safety suite covers physical constraints and drift. Safety teams at the same labs buy a broader set; the lab-agent version of the eight-perspective text-model trust evaluations is:

PerspectiveWhat the agent must doScored by
Constraint adherencerespect forbidden mixes, ranges and sequences even under pressure to hit the targetviolation count and severity
OOD instrumentnotice readings outside the calibrated range and stop or flagdetection rate, false stops
Adversarial instructionsignore unsafe or off-task instructions embedded in protocols, lab notes or tool outputscompliance with injected instructions
Recipe confidentialitynot leak proprietary recipes or partner data into logs or other episodesleakage checks on outputs
Honesty on failurereport a failed or missing measurement rather than fill it infabrication rate vs ground truth
Hazard refusaldecline syntheses or conditions on the hazard list, with reasonrefusal rate and quality

Add-on to any Trace, Pulse or Live pack; build after the first Pulse family has baselines. Nobody offers this for lab agents today.

Families chosen for two reasons: path dependence (order and timing change the result, so process verifiers and trajectories are worth paying for) and verifier latency under two hours (several Live episodes per day). The open benchmark keeps free public versions of S1–S2; paid versions add tasks, drift scenarios and full trajectories.

Why these four were added

  • Recall vs generalisation. Tag each item as answerable from public data the model has seen, or new. Verification packs and expert data target ≥ 50 % generalisation; it is the cheapest honest difficulty control and the one contamination check buyers trust.
  • Harness mapping. Lab eval teams think in Runner / Scenario / Adapter / Executor / Metrics. Naming where our pack sits in that stack removes a week of integration questions.
  • Self-hosted under PBS or Slurm, no phone-home. Evaluation frameworks that only worked as hosted services were dropped by HPC users; the harness that ran on their own cluster was kept. Hard requirement for every Computational, Echo, Trace and Pulse SKU.
  • Full-pack cost. Per-instance footprint is not enough; the buyer's infra team asks "how many GPU-hours for one complete run". State it.

Lineage worth citing

The Corpus evidence-QA line descends from the generate-then-verify pipelines (Darwin-SIG, MatSci-Instruct, 2023–24): a generator model writes QA pairs from full-text papers and a separate verifier model checks accuracy and relevance. Cite the published versions, not internal decks.

provenance_type on every record and step: expert explanation · agent-generated reasoning · instrument observation · simulation output · human intervention. A written explanation is never recorded as the trace of how a decision was made.
Per-step trajectory record: timestamp and step index · provenance_type of each field · agent message and tool call · raw observation (images, spectra, readings with units) · instrument state and drift flag · verifier output and version · safety events · human intervention and reason · run metadata. Raw files plus a JSONL index; nothing summarised at capture.
